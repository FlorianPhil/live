# Toneoptic camera grammar (all future shots)

Every Toneoptic shot is one of two modes only:

1. **Static** — locked-off / hold. No pan, no tilt wander, no handheld drift. Focus racks are OK if the body stays planted.
2. **Circular orbit** — camera travels on a **perfect circle**, as if mounted on the tip of a clock hand. Constant radius around a fixed center (usually the product or the action). Speed is smooth and even. The path is a pure arc / full 360 / partial clock-sweep — never a straight dolly, never a vertical crane-only move, never shaky handheld.

What “rotating” means here: **the camera orbits**. It does **not** mean the product spins, and it does **not** mean the Toneoptic **rpm** mechanism. The **can** stays still in space (unless a human hand physically slides/carries it). Orbit angle can be high (top-down clock), mid (table-height), or low (floor-skimming) — different clock planes, same perfect circle.

Allowed pairs:
- Static → cut → static
- Static → cut → circular orbit
- One continuous circular orbit (partial or full)
- Circular orbit that settles into a static hold at the end of the arc

Forbidden camera: linear push-ins as the only move, lateral trucks that aren’t on a circular path, chaotic handheld, product turntables / lazy-susan spins unless explicitly requested.
